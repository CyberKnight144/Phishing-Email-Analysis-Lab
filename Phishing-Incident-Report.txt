
PHISHING EMAIL INCIDENT REPORT

Analyst: Elmar Sims
Investigation Date: September 13, 2026
Incident Type: Phishing Email
Severity: High
Status: Investigated
Final Classification: Phishing - Credential Harvesting
Confidence: High

--------------------------------------------------
1. INCIDENT SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------

A suspicious email claiming to originate from Microsoft 365 Security
was analyzed after being identified as a potential phishing message.

The email attempted to pressure the recipient into verifying their
Microsoft 365 account by threatening account suspension within
24 hours.

Analysis identified multiple phishing indicators, including a
lookalike sender domain, suspicious credential-verification URL,
failed SPF and DMARC authentication, absence of DKIM authentication,
and urgency-based social engineering.

Based on the combined evidence, the message was classified as a
high-confidence credential phishing attempt.

--------------------------------------------------
3. INITIAL TRIAGE
--------------------------------------------------

During initial review, the email displayed several characteristics
commonly associated with phishing and social-engineering attacks.

Finding 1 - Lookalike Sender Domain:
The sender used "micros0ft-support.example," replacing the letter
"o" in Microsoft with the number "0." This is consistent with a
typosquatting technique intended to make a fraudulent domain appear
similar to a legitimate brand.

Finding 2 - Urgency and Fear:
The subject and message threatened that the recipient's Microsoft 365
account would be suspended within 24 hours. This language attempts to
pressure the recipient into making a rushed decision.

Finding 3 - Suspicious Verification URL:
The email instructed the recipient to verify the account using
"https://login-microsoft365.example/verify." The URL contains Microsoft
branding but does not use an official Microsoft-owned domain.

Finding 4 - Credential-Theft Indicators:
The message attempted to direct the recipient to an external
verification page. Based on the context of the email, the likely
objective was to convince the recipient to enter account credentials.

Initial Assessment:
Suspicious - Further investigation required.


--------------------------------------------------
4. HEADER AND AUTHENTICATION ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------

The email headers were reviewed to determine the originating
infrastructure and email authentication results.

Received Header:
The message indicated that it originated from:

mail.micros0ft-support.example [192.0.2.45]

The IP address 192.0.2.45 belongs to the TEST-NET-1 documentation
range and is being used as a controlled indicator for this lab.

SPF Analysis:
Result: FAIL

The sending server was not authorized under the SPF policy evaluated
for the sender identity. This authentication failure increased the
suspicion associated with the message.

DKIM Analysis:
Result: NONE

No DKIM authentication was present for the message. The absence of
DKIM alone does not prove that an email is malicious, but it provided
additional context when combined with the other indicators.

DMARC Analysis:
Result: FAIL

DMARC authentication/alignment requirements were not satisfied for
the domain presented in the From header.

Authentication Summary:

SPF:   FAIL
DKIM:  NONE
DMARC: FAIL

Analyst Assessment:
The authentication results, when correlated with the lookalike sender
domain, suspicious URL, and social-engineering content, further
supported the phishing classification.

--------------------------------------------------
6. FINAL ANALYST VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------

Final Classification:
PHISHING - CREDENTIAL HARVESTING

Severity:
HIGH

Confidence:
HIGH

Likely Objective:
Credential Theft

Analyst Verdict:

The email was determined to be a simulated credential-phishing
attempt designed to impersonate Microsoft 365 Security and pressure
the recipient into visiting a fraudulent account-verification page.

The classification was based on multiple correlated indicators:

- Lookalike sender domain using "micros0ft" instead of "microsoft"
- Suspicious Microsoft-themed verification URL
- SPF authentication failure
- No DKIM authentication present
- DMARC authentication failure
- Urgency and account-suspension language
- Attempt to direct the recipient to an external verification page

VirusTotal did not establish malicious reputation for the controlled
lab indicators. However, absence of reputation data does not override
the behavioral, authentication, and social-engineering evidence
identified during the investigation.

Final Disposition:
TRUE POSITIVE - PHISHING



--------------------------------------------------
7. RECOMMENDED RESPONSE ACTIONS
--------------------------------------------------

Based on the investigation findings, the following response actions
are recommended:

1. Email Containment
   - Quarantine or remove the phishing email from the recipient's
     mailbox.
   - Search the email environment for additional recipients who may
     have received the same or similar message.

2. Indicator Blocking
   - Block confirmed malicious sender addresses, domains, and URLs
     using appropriate email and security controls.
   - Do not block 192.0.2.45 based on this lab investigation because
     it is a reserved TEST-NET-1 documentation address.

3. User Investigation
   - Determine whether the recipient clicked the suspicious URL.
   - Determine whether credentials or other sensitive information
     were submitted.

4. Account Containment
   - If credential compromise is confirmed, reset the affected user's
     password.
   - Revoke active sessions and investigate the account for
     unauthorized access.
   - Review authentication activity for suspicious logins.

5. Threat Hunting
   - Search security logs and email telemetry for the identified
     phishing indicators and similar messages.
   - Investigate any additional affected users or endpoints.

6. User Awareness
   - Inform the affected user of the phishing attempt.
   - Reinforce reporting procedures and awareness of lookalike
     domains, suspicious links, and urgency-based social engineering.
